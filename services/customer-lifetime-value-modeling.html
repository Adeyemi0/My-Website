Common Questions About CLV Modeling

What's the difference between historical and predictive CLV?
Historical CLV looks backward: "How much did past customers spend on average?" Predictive CLV looks forward: "What will this specific customer spend over the next 12-24 months based on their behavior, cohort, and churn probability?" Predictive models enable proactive budget allocation and retention strategy, while historical averages only explain the past.
How much customer data do I need to start?
Ideally 12+ months of transactional or subscription data. However, I can often build a functional MVP with 6 months if your data is clean and well-structured. During the discovery call, I'll assess your data readiness and tell you exactly what's needed for reliable predictions.
How do we measure the ROI of a CLV project?
We track three primary metrics: (1) Improvement in LTV:CAC ratio by channel, (2) Reduction in payback period, and (3) Revenue saved/recovered through targeted retention. Most clients see a clear ROI within 60-90 days of implementing the budget reallocations I recommend.
Can this work for both one-time purchase and subscription businesses?
Yes. I use different modeling approaches depending on your revenue model: BG/NBD + Gamma-Gamma for transactional/e-commerce, and survival analysis/cohort decay for SaaS/subscriptions. The framework adapts to your business model while maintaining the same strategic output: predictable customer value and optimized spend.
Who should be involved in the project?
For maximum impact, I recommend involving: a data/tech lead (for data access), a marketing/growth lead (for channel spend context), and a finance/strategy lead (for budget allocation). I run a single kickoff call to align everyone, then handle the heavy lifting independently with weekly check-ins.
